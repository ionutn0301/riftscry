World and architecture

A split chronology causeway carries sixteen release stations. Asymmetric beveled ribs, brass rails, stone foundations and framed official champion art form one persistent place. Stations use equal release-order spacing, not elapsed time. Selecting a champion moves its bay and the camera; My Pool reorganizes bays; comparison unfolds selected stations and raises endpoint brackets. Search lives beside, never over, the scene.

atlas-state.ts owns view, champion, patch, interval, filters, pool projection and modes. URL parsing and serialization use that same state. RiftAtlas.tsx provides semantic controls; AtlasWorld.tsx consumes the state without creating a separate data model. Camera travel retargets from its current pose instead of queuing animations. Reader mode preserves canonical routes and the existing local-device pool store. No account service, database or new dependency was introduced.

Controls: navigation buttons, full-roster search, chronology buttons/slider, two comparison endpoints, pool add/remove, global search (Ctrl/Cmd K), Reduce motion and Reader view. Comparison preserves the repository’s older-exclusive/newer-inclusive recorded-change semantics, including reversed endpoints. It does not reconstruct historical champion statistics. Coverage remains 26.1–26.16, not the current live patch.

Measured budgets and limitations

  • Final captured scene: at most 188 render calls, 20,518 triangles and 7 GPU textures in the tested four-champion/one-member-pool flow; measured DPR 1. Device cap is 1.25.
  • Repeated architecture is instanced; idle rendering is demand-driven; hidden-tab rendering pauses. Collection art staging is limited to twelve pool members plus the curated/selected identities. Larger collections were not profiled.
  • Production gzip sizes: homepage HTML 72,457 bytes; AtlasWorld chunk 245,964 bytes; RiftAtlas chunk 6,445 bytes. These are individual files, not total page transfer (shared chunks and external art are additional).
  • Development lab at /, remote Chromium: FCP/LCP 1,712 ms; TTFB 1,521.9 ms; CLS 0. INP and hydration timing unavailable. LCP is the heading, not scene readiness. These are not production field metrics.
  • SwiftShader video and frame-delta telemetry do not establish a physical-GPU frame-rate target. Demand-loop deltas include idle time and must not be interpreted directly as FPS. Mid-range Android, iPhone Safari, thermal behavior and large-pool budgets remain unverified.

Not signed off: premium-reference visual equivalence, physical-device smoothness, Safari execution, comprehensive accessibility certification and production/commercial rights eligibility. No production deployment was performed. Review these concrete gaps rather than treating a successful build or a video as blanket completion.
